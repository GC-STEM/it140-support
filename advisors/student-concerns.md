# IT 140 Advisors | Student Concerns and Conversations

Students may contact their advisor first because the advisor is often a familiar, long-term point of contact.

The advisor does not need to solve the programming or technical problem to help the student make progress.

## A Useful Advisor Conversation

When a student says they are having trouble in IT 140, ask enough to identify the category.

A simple sequence is:

1. **What are you trying to do?**
2. **What is stopping you?**
3. **Is the problem with the course technology, understanding the programming, the assignment requirements, or something broader?**
4. **Have you contacted the instructor or another support resource yet?**
5. **Can you continue working in the CVD while a local-computer issue is being resolved?**

The goal is classification and connection, not technical diagnosis.

## "I'm Not a Computer Person"

Students can enter IT 140 with very different levels of technical experience.

A useful response is to distinguish:

- **learning programming**, which is the purpose of the course; from
- **making the course technology function**, which should not require the student to solve unsupported technical problems alone.

For programming learning:

- encourage the student to contact their instructor;
- point to LSS support;
- encourage use of course practice/resources.

For a technology failure:

- route to the IT Service Desk.

## "I'm Falling Behind"

Programming concepts build on earlier material, so early support matters.

Help the student identify what is causing the delay:

### Technical Barrier

Examples:

- cannot access CVD;
- cannot install/use the supported environment;
- GitHub/repository workflow is failing.

**Next step:** IT Service Desk.

### Learning Barrier

Examples:

- does not understand loops;
- cannot translate pseudocode into code;
- is struggling to debug.

**Next step:** faculty and/or LSS.

### Assignment/Communication Barrier

Examples:

- unclear about a requirement;
- unsure what to submit;
- confused by instructor feedback.

**Next step:** faculty.

### Broader Academic Concern

Examples:

- workload;
- course sequencing;
- competing responsibilities;
- concern about staying enrolled or academic progress.

**Next step:** advisor using normal advising practices, while also connecting the course-specific barrier to the right support role.

## "I Can't Install the Software"

A local installation is optional when the CVD is available.

The advisor can tell the student:

- IT 140 provides a browser-accessed CVD reference environment;
- the student can use the CVD rather than continue struggling with a local installation; and
- the IT Service Desk can help with technical access/environment problems.

Do not walk the student through manual software installation.

## "I Don't Have the Right Computer"

Do not immediately advise the student that a new computer is required for IT 140 solely because the local setup will not run.

The student may be able to use the CVD.

Route device/access-specific questions to the IT Service Desk and use [Technology Context](technology-context.md) for the course environment model.

## "I Don't Understand the Assignment"

The advisor does not need to interpret programming assignment requirements.

Direct the student to:

1. the current D2L Brightspace Guidelines and Rubric; and
2. their instructor for requirement or grading interpretation.

If the difficulty is understanding the underlying programming concept rather than the requirement itself, faculty or LSS can provide learning support.

## "My Instructor Hasn't Fixed My Computer Problem"

Clarify the role boundary.

Faculty are responsible for instruction, assignment interpretation, grading, and instructor feedback.

Technical environment/access failures belong to the IT Service Desk.

The student may need both:

- technical support to restore the environment; and
- faculty communication about the academic impact.

## "I Tried Help, but I'm Still Stuck"

Identify which support has already been used and whether the problem changed.

For example:

- Service Desk fixed Python, but the student still does not understand the code → faculty/LSS.
- LSS helped with loops, but GitHub authentication still fails → Service Desk.
- Technical and learning issues are resolved, but the student remains concerned about academic progress → advisor.

Avoid sending the student back and forth without identifying the unresolved layer.

## Avoid Overpromising

Advisors should not promise:

- a particular grade outcome;
- an instructor decision;
- that a technical problem will be fixed in a specific way;
- that a particular unsupported device will work; or
- that a student may ignore current course requirements because of a technical issue.

Instead, connect the student to the role authorized to make that determination.
