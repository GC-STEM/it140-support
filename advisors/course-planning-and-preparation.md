# IT 140 Advisors | Course Planning and Preparation

Use this page when helping a student prepare for IT 140 or decide how IT 140 fits with other courses in an upcoming term.

The goal is to help the student begin the term with realistic expectations and useful preparation **without asking the student to begin IT 140 course work before the official term start**.

## Set the Workload Expectation Early

IT 140 is a three-credit course delivered in eight weeks. Under SNHU and applicable accreditation expectations, students should plan for **about 16 hours per week on average for an average student**.

Students with no previous programming experience may need more time in some weeks.

The zyBooks portion of the workload is significant throughout the term. As a planning estimate:

- early weeks may require approximately **4–5 hours per week** in zyBooks; and
- later weeks may require approximately **2–3 hours per week** in zyBooks.

The remainder of the weekly workload includes other course content, programming practice, assignments/projects, testing, debugging, review, and support.

## Help the Student Plan the Week, Not Just the Total Hours

Programming work benefits from repeated cycles of learning, practice, testing, debugging, and help-seeking.

A useful default pattern is to begin early in the week and work approximately **2–2.5 hours per day**, adjusted for the specific module, due dates, and the student's circumstances.

When the student needs help deciding what to do first, a useful general priority is:

1. current **zyBooks Participation Activities and Lab Activities**;
2. the current **module assignment or milestone**; and
3. if planned time remains, continue the active **Text-Adventure Game project**.

Current Brightspace requirements and due dates always take precedence over this general planning pattern.

Students who need help turning this into a workable routine can be referred to [Academic Coaching](../academic-support/coaching.md#time-and-task-prioritization).

## Help the Student Plan the Rest of the Schedule

Do not use a blanket rule that every student must avoid mathematics or another technical course while taking IT 140.

Instead, consider the student's **combined workload**. Students who are new to programming may benefit from balancing IT 140 with courses that place fewer simultaneous programming-, mathematics-, laboratory-, or project-intensive demands on them.

Advisor feedback identifies concurrent mathematics courses, DAT-220, and other coding courses as combinations that may require especially careful workload planning.

Use normal advising policy and the student's program requirements when recommending a schedule.

## Optional Preparation Before the Term

Appropriate optional preparation may include:

- reviewing basic Python concepts with non-graded preparatory resources;
- reviewing general programming/problem-solving resources;
- checking that the student has a device and browser that can access required course systems;
- reviewing the current IT 140 technology requirements;
- learning what the terms Python, IDE, CVD, VS Code, zyBooks, Git, and GitHub mean at a high level; and
- locating instructor and Academic Support options before they are urgently needed.

For terminology, use the [IT 140 Course Glossary](../shared/glossary.md).

For current environment information, use [Technology Context for Advisors](technology-context.md) and [Supported Environments](../shared/supported-environments.md).

For Academic Support options, use [Academic Support for IT 140](../academic-support/README.md).

### Do Not Recommend Starting Course Work Early

Optional preparation is not the same as completing IT 140 work before the course begins.

Advisors should **not** tell students to begin or complete:

- graded assignments or projects;
- zyBooks assignments for the upcoming term;
- Module One course tasks;
- discussion work; or
- other required IT 140 course work

before the official term start unless current university/course guidance explicitly directs otherwise.

## Use the Two-Week Preview Period for Orientation

When the course is available during the two-week preview period, encourage the student to use that access to **orient and prepare**, not to begin course work early.

Useful preview-period activities include:

- locate the weekly modules;
- review the syllabus/course expectations;
- identify where Brightspace provides zyBooks access directions;
- note the course technology requirements;
- locate instructor and support information;
- identify the current Guidelines and Rubric locations; and
- plan blocks of time for the first week.

The preview period can reduce surprises without changing the official start of course work.

## Students With Previous Programming Experience

Students with prior Python or programming experience may benefit from a light review before the term.

Encourage review of fundamentals rather than assuming prior experience will make the course effortless. Students still need to follow the current IT 140 course workflow, tools, assignment requirements, and submission directions.

## Students With No Programming Experience

No previous programming experience is required for IT 140.

Advisor feedback identifies **CS-205 may be a helpful preparatory option** for some students with no coding experience when the student has appropriate free-elective space.

Treat CS-205 as an **optional advising consideration, not an IT 140 prerequisite or universal recommendation**. Confirm the student's current program requirements and available elective space before recommending an additional course.

Students who do not take a preparatory course can still prepare by reviewing basic Python/programming resources and planning to use instructor and Academic Support resources early.

## Academic Support to Identify Before the Term

Use [Academic Support for IT 140](../academic-support/README.md) for the current F&S-facing service map.

Depending on the student's needs, useful options may include:

- **24/7 Drop-In Tutoring** for on-demand general Python help;
- **IT 140 - Intro to Python Workshop**;
- **IT Basics Office Hours**;
- **Academic Coaching** for time/task planning, organization, technical-reading strategies, academic skills, and learning strategies;
- **Written Feedback** for appropriate writing-development needs; and
- **ELL/ESOL support pathways**.

The current-term group-session schedule is available at:

[Group Sessions Schedule: Workshops, Office Hours, and Peer Groups](https://snhuacademicresourcecenter.screenstepslive.com/a/1834032-group-sessions-schedule-workshops-office-hours-and-peer-groups)

Schedules can change each term, so use the current page rather than giving students memorized days/times.

## Recommended Advisor Message

A useful expectation to communicate is:

> IT 140 does not require previous programming experience, but it is a fast-paced eight-week programming course. Plan for about 16 hours per week on average, spread your work across the week, and use your instructor and the appropriate Academic Support service early if you begin to struggle.
